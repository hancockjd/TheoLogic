Terms of Use

Draft — no effective date

Structure only — requires counsel These headings are the sections this site's terms need. The text under each says what the section must establish. It is drafting guidance, not contract language, and none of it is enforceable as written. Theo Logic's attorney should draft the operative terms.

1. Acceptance

State who is bound, from what moment, and what happens if someone does not agree. Set a minimum age.

2. Who may use the site

Eligibility, and any conduct that forfeits access.

3. Intellectual property in site content

Assert ownership of the site's text, design, film stills, music, and marks, and define the narrow license granted to a visitor. Note that the imagery presently on the site is placeholder artwork; when real production stills replace it, confirm the company actually holds the rights to publish each one, including any talent and location releases.

4. Unsolicited submissions

The most important section on this page for a production company. It must state clearly that unsolicited ideas, scripts, and materials sent outside The Vault are not accepted in confidence and create no obligation — and it must not contradict what The Vault promises. The two documents have to be read together and agree. Detailed terms belong in the Submission Policy.

5. Acceptable use

Prohibit scraping, interference, reverse engineering, unauthorized access, and unlawful use.

6. Third-party links and services

Disclaim responsibility for destinations the site links to, and name the third-party services a visitor may be handed off to — e-signature, payment processing, streaming.

7. Purchases

Required before the store transacts. Cover pricing, taxes, payment authorization, delivery of digital goods, shipping of physical goods, refunds, and cancellations. The store is not yet connected to any payment provider; this section must be complete and accurate before it is.

8. Disclaimers

Standard "as is" disclaimer, scoped to what the law of the governing jurisdiction permits.

9. Limitation of liability

Cap and exclusions, again within what is permitted.

10. Indemnification

What a user covers the company for.

11. Governing law and disputes

Choice of law, venue, and whether arbitration or a class-action waiver applies. If arbitration is used, the clause needs conspicuous presentation to be enforceable.

12. Changes to these terms

How amendments take effect and how users are notified.

13. Contact

Where legal notices are sent. Confirm a real address for service of notice.
