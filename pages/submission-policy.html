Submission Policy

Draft — no effective date

Structure only — requires counsel This is the highest-risk document on the site. A production company that receives outside creative material and later releases something similar invites an idea-submission claim; this policy and the NDA it references are the primary defense. The headings below describe what the document must establish. The text is guidance, not legal language.

Do not publish until The Vault exists The home page describes encrypted intake, a countersigned NDA before anyone reads a word, hashed and timestamped records, per-reader access grants, and a scheduled purge. None of that is built. Publishing a policy that promises those controls, before the systems perform them, creates real exposure. Either build the system first or revise both this page and the home page copy to describe what actually happens.

1. What we accept

Define the accepted formats and channels, and state that The Vault is the only route for submissions. Material arriving by any other means — email, social, mail — must be addressed by section 2.

2. Unsolicited material sent outside The Vault

State that such material is not accepted in confidence, is not reviewed, creates no obligation and no relationship, and may be discarded unread. This must agree with the corresponding section of the Terms of Use.

3. The agreement that governs a submission

Describe the mutual NDA and submission release: when it is presented, when it takes effect, who countersigns, and that no reader opens the material before both signatures exist. Make clear which document controls if this policy and the executed agreement ever conflict.

4. What we do with a submission

Describe intake, storage, who may read it, and the retention and purge schedule. Every promise here has to match a system behavior that can be evidenced later — an audit log is only a defense if it exists and is produced.

5. Independent development

The clause that matters most. Reserve the company's right to develop, acquire, or release material independently created or received from other sources, including work similar to a submission. Without this, any later project in an adjacent space is exposed.

6. No obligation and no compensation

State that review creates no obligation to proceed, no partnership, and no entitlement to payment or credit unless a separate signed agreement says otherwise.

7. Your representations

Require the submitter to warrant that the work is theirs, that they hold the rights to submit it, that it does not infringe, and that any co-authors or rights-holders have consented.

8. Chain of title

For adapted material, life rights, or optioned work, state the documentation required. The home page already advertises "documented chain of title" under Story Development, so define the standard being claimed.

9. Decisions

Describe the possible outcomes — pass, hold, option — the target response window, and how a decision is communicated and recorded.

10. Minors

State whether submissions from minors are accepted, and if so what parental or guardian consent is required. Relevant given the KINDNESS Project's audience.

11. Contact

Where submission questions go. vault@theologic.com is an unconfirmed placeholder.
