Privacy Policy

Draft — no effective date

Structure only — requires counsel The headings below are the sections a privacy policy for this site needs. The text under each describes what that section must establish; it is drafting guidance, not legal language and not a policy. Theo Logic's attorney should write the operative text.

The obligations depend on facts not yet settled: where the company operates, where visitors are, whether analytics run, and what the inquiry form and Vault actually do with what they receive. Those answers determine whether GDPR, CCPA/CPRA, or other regimes apply.

What the site does today. As currently deployed, this site runs no analytics, sets no cookies, and uses no third-party embeds. The inquiry form is not connected to a backend, so nothing a visitor types is transmitted or stored. This paragraph stops being true the moment the form is wired up or analytics are added — revise it then.

1. Who we are

Identify the legal entity (Theo Logic, LLC), its jurisdiction of formation, its place of business, and the contact route for privacy questions. Name a data controller and, if one is required, a representative or data protection officer.

2. Information we collect

Enumerate each category actually collected, and no more. Expected categories once the site is live:

  • Information you give us — name, email, organization, and message body submitted through the inquiry form.
  • Submission material — anything sent through The Vault, which is a separate and more sensitive category. See the Submission Policy.
  • Automatically collected — server logs, IP address, user agent. State what the host retains, including GitHub Pages or whichever host is used at launch.
  • Analytics — none at present. If added, name the provider and what it collects.

3. How we use information

State each purpose, tied to the categories above. Do not use a catch-all. If any processing is automated decision-making or profiling — relevant to the claims the Intelligence Layer section makes on the home page — say so explicitly and describe it.

4. Legal bases for processing

Required if any visitor may be in the UK or EEA. Map each purpose to a basis: consent, contract, legal obligation, or legitimate interests. If legitimate interests, record the balancing test.

5. Sharing and disclosure

Name the categories of recipient: hosting, email, e-signature platform, payment processor if the store goes live, and any professional advisors. State plainly whether personal information is ever sold or shared for cross-context behavioral advertising — a specific disclosure under CCPA/CPRA.

6. Cookies and similar technologies

The site currently sets no cookies and uses no local storage. If that changes, describe each cookie, its purpose and lifespan, and how consent is obtained where consent is required.

7. Data retention

State how long each category is kept and what triggers deletion. The home page already commits publicly to a purge schedule for passed submissions; whatever is promised there must match what this section says and what the company actually does.

8. Your rights

Describe the applicable rights — access, correction, deletion, portability, objection, restriction, and the right to withdraw consent — and give a working mechanism for exercising them, with a response timeframe. Include the CCPA/CPRA notices if California residents are served, and the right to lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority under GDPR.

9. Security

Describe safeguards accurately and in general terms. Take care here: the home page currently advertises encryption in transit and at rest, isolated storage, and tamper-evident audit logging. Any security statement must describe systems that exist, or it becomes a misrepresentation.

10. Children's privacy

State the minimum age for using the site and submitting material. This deserves real attention given the KINDNESS Project's classroom-ready media and the likelihood of minors in the audience — COPPA may apply.

11. International transfers

If data moves across borders, identify the destinations and the transfer mechanism relied upon.

12. Changes to this policy

Explain how changes are communicated and how the effective date is versioned.

13. Contact

Give a monitored address for privacy requests and a postal address if one is required. hello@theologic.com is an unconfirmed placeholder.
