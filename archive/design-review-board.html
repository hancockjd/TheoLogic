Two directions, one decision

Is Theo Logic a film company that happens to do good, or a mission that happens to use film?

Both concepts below are complete, working websites — same content, same functional requirements, two different answers to that one question. Palette, typography, imagery, even how the secure idea exchange gets explained: everything else follows from the answer. So the useful thing to decide here isn't which one you like. It's which one is the company you're actually building.

Before you open them

Three things to know

The photography isn't real yet

Every image in both concepts is placeholder artwork — light, sky, and architecture, composed in each direction's palette. None of it claims anything about your portfolio. Real stills, production photography, and headshots replace it during the build.

They are identical in substance

Same mission, vision, motto, and tagline, word for word from you. Same four capabilities — secure idea exchange, e-signature, the engagement layer, and commerce. Nothing is in one concept and missing from the other.

The choice doesn't change the cost

Scope, sequencing, and build effort are the same either way. Pick the one that's right for the company, not the one that looks cheaper or more ambitious — neither is.

Our position

What we'd recommend, and why

Direction B — and it isn't close.

Your own material makes the argument. The mission leads with "informs, inspires, and encourages positive societal change." The vision is built on the KINDNESS Project. The tagline is literally an invitation — You Too. That's a company whose differentiator is why it makes things, and Direction B puts that on the first screen. Direction A buries it below the fold.

There's a practical argument too. Direction A is the house style of every production company website in the country. Direction B looks like almost nothing else in your category — and standing apart is worth more than any single design decision on the page.

Where Direction A wins: if the money in the next eighteen months is commercial and branded work, the dark treatment reads as more expensive and closes that buyer faster.

A third option worth putting on the table

Run B as the company site and let individual film and project pages go dark and cinematic. Warm front door, dark rooms. It's common in well-run media companies, it lets each film carry its own mood, and it costs almost nothing extra — it's a palette switch per page template, not a second design system.

How to decide

Four questions worth more than a preference

"Which do you like better" gets you a vote and no reasoning. These get you an answer you can defend six months from now.

1

Who has to say yes for this site to have done its job?

A distributor and a network point to A. A funder, a sponsor, or a school district points to B.

2

Someone lands here for four seconds. What do they walk away knowing?

That you're excellent, or that you're on a mission. Both are true. Only one can go first.

3

Where does the revenue come from over the next eighteen months?

Commercial and branded work points to A. Grants, sponsorship, institutional licensing, and direct sales point to B.

4

Look at the two opening screens with the sound off. Which one is the company you're building?

Not the one you'd rather look at. The one that's accurate.

Regardless of the choice

What both concepts already handle

  • Mission, vision, motto, and tagline used verbatim — no paraphrasing, no reinterpretation
  • Secure idea and IP exchange, presented alongside the e-signature step
  • The AI engagement layer, labeled honestly as illustrative sample data rather than real numbers
  • Commerce for film and audio merchandise, with Stripe, Visa, Mastercard, and PayPal
  • Zelle handled as invoiced orders only — it has no checkout API and no chargeback protection, so it should never be a storefront button
  • Responsive on phone and tablet, keyboard navigable, and readable to screen readers
  • Self-contained single files with no external dependencies — nothing to break, nothing to license
Working backward

The path to November 2

You have an investor meeting on November 2. A live site by then is a different kind of proof than a deck — it's the company existing in public. Ten weeks is enough, but only about six of them are build weeks. Here's what that sequence looks like.

Aug 21 – 28

Your team reviews both directions

Open each concept, sit with the four questions above, and come to the next conversation with a leaning rather than a verdict.

Week of Sep 1

Working session — direction chosen

Ninety minutes. We work through the four questions together, settle the direction, and clear the open scope items: the e-signature product, the merchandise question, and where the logo and brand marks stand.

Sep 1 – 12

Definition and content inventory

Sitemap, page-by-page scope, and a written list of every piece of content we'll need from you — bios, stills, the film's description, the KINDNESS Project story. This is the step that protects the date.

Sep 15 – Oct 17

Build

The public site: mission, the KINDNESS Project, the film, the team, and contact. The Vault, the engagement layer, and the store follow in a later phase — the Vault in particular is gated on a submission release and NDA from entertainment counsel, which is a legal timeline we don't control.

Oct 19 – 26

Content load, review, revisions

Real copy and real imagery in place, your review, and one round of changes.

Oct 28

Live, with a week of buffer

Deliberately not November 1.

Nov 2

Investor meeting

Anyone in that room who looks up Theo Logic finds an operating company.

The one thing that reliably moves a date like this is content. Bios, photographs, and approved copy take longer to gather than anyone expects. If the direction is settled in the first week of September and content starts arriving alongside the build, November 2 is comfortable rather than tight.
